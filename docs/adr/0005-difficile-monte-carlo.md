# Difficile plays cards by Monte-Carlo determinisation, with Moyen as its playout policy

Difficile keeps Moyen's bidding, king call, écart and poignée, and only chooses its cards differently. For each card it may play, it draws sampled deals (full distributions of the hidden cards) that fit everything its seat knows: cards played, colours others lack, shown poignées and écart trumps, the revealed chien, and a known partner. In each sampled deal it plays the card, has Moyen play the rest of the deal at every seat, and keeps the card with the best average final score for its seat. The same sampled deals judge every card. Their number is set by a budget of simulated cards: each sampled deal costs one playout per playable card, each as long as the cards left in the deal, and a decision plays at most 150 cards in all (and 20 sampled deals). So a seed always gives the same decision, and a decision takes a few milliseconds, inside the 10 ms of CPU a request gets on Cloudflare's free Workers plan. Early in a deal not even one sampled deal fits, and Difficile plays Moyen's card. A time budget was rejected: it varies with the machine, and tests could not reproduce its decisions.

Playing out a sampled deal needs the whole history of the deal, which the redacted view does not hold (only the last trick). So the bot memory records every completed trick, and the room server rebuilds it from the public tricks for a bot standing in mid-deal. The engine builds a validated deal in play from that history and one sampled distribution (`createDealInPlay`), so the bots never assemble engine state by hand.

## Consequences

- The budget was first 200 playouts per decision (up to ~360 ms). It was cut to 150 simulated cards so the room server fits Cloudflare's free plan. Benchmark against Moyen, 150 paired deals: +2.5 a deal at 3 players, +4.2 at 4, +8.8 at 5, against +12, +22 and +9 with the former budget (60 deals). A whole bot turn of the room server (the decision, playing it, the views sent, the room serialised for storage) took 4.3 ms at the 99th percentile and 8.4 ms at worst (a cold start), on a laptop, with Difficile bots at 3, 4 and 5 players; a test checks that no decision plays more simulated cards than the budget. A paid plan would allow raising `SIMULATED_CARDS` (`packages/bots/src/hard/budget.ts`); `BENCH_SIMULATED_CARDS` tries a budget in the benchmark.
- In 5 players Difficile plays like Moyen until the partner is known. Each sampled deal settles the partner differently, and choosing a card that is best in each such sampled deal lost about 12 points a deal against Moyen. Waiting turned that into a gain (benchmark with the former budget of 200 playouts, paired deals: +12 at 3 players, +22 at 4, +9 at 5).
- Sampled deals do not use every inference the table allows. Levers if Difficile needs to be stronger: the bidding (a taker is likely strong), an Excuse shown in a poignée (its owner has no other trump), trumps shown in the écart (the taker had nothing else to discard).
- The benchmark plays each deal twice on the same cards, once with Moyen in Difficile's seats, because the score of single deals is too noisy. It takes minutes and runs on demand (`pnpm --filter @tarot/bots bench`), not in the normal test suite.
